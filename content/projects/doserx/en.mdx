# DoseRx

## The problem

A doctor works out a weight-based dose for every patient. Multiply the dose per kilogram by the weight, divide by the number of doses, convert to millilitres, and check against the maximum. For children, the dose depends on weight and a mistake can do harm. The usual tools are paper references, a phone calculator or a spreadsheet. None of them is quick to use with one hand at the bedside.

DoseRx is built for one practising doctor who works on a phone at the bedside and on a desktop. It needs no accounts and no server.

## What I built

### The maths lives in one place, apart from the screens

I kept the dose calculation separate from the interface. It takes typed inputs, checks them, applies the dose caps and returns either a result or an error. A screen has to check which one it got before it shows a number. So a failed calculation can never appear as a dose, and a broken value never reaches the doctor. The same engine serves every calculator and is tested on its own.

### Doses snap to what an Indonesian pharmacy sells

After calculating a dose, the app suggests the tablet fraction or syrup volume to give, using preparations sold in Indonesian pharmacies. Tablets snap to fractions a person can cut: ¼, ½, ¾, 1, 1½ and 2. The app shows the dose actually delivered next to the target, so the doctor sees any rounding. The Puyer tab does the same for compounded powders. It calculates each drug in a batch and prints a combined recipe card with tablet counts and signa.

### The catalog grew from 8 drugs to 92

The spec started with 8 seed drugs. The catalog now has 92 across 16 categories. Each has a dose range, maximum caps, stock concentration and a dosing reference such as IDAI, BNFc, WHO or Fornas. I split the catalog into seven modules and kept reference details apart from the calculation fields. Side effects, contraindications and pack sizes can grow without touching the maths. A collapsible "Detail Obat" panel shows them when tapped.

### Four calculators for four jobs

Preset uses the catalog. Kustom lets the doctor save their own drugs and reuse them. Puyer handles compounded powders. Infus calculates IV drip rates in millilitres per hour and drops per minute, with its own engine and four dose units. A weight estimator uses the APLS and Luscombe-Owens paediatric formulas and labels its answer as an estimate.

### The doctor can adjust a preset and see that it changed

The first plan used preset doses as they were. In practice a doctor sometimes needs a different dose per kilogram within the valid range. When a preset dose changes, an amber marker and a reset button appear. My first version didn't pass the changed dose into the calculation, and I fixed that right after.

## Result

DoseRx is live on GitHub Pages and installs to a phone's home screen. It works offline once loaded. Recent and starred drugs sit at the top of the picker, so the 92-drug list stays quick to use with one hand. Every calculation is saved in the browser with an optional note. The original spec had 8 drugs and three panels. Over about three and a half weeks in June and July 2026, I built four calculators, a history drawer and the full catalog. The app states that it is a calculation aid and not clinical decision support. That notice stays visible.

## Under the hood

- React 18 and TypeScript on Vite, with `vite-plugin-pwa`. Plain CSS. No UI, state or router library.
- `src/lib/calculate.ts` has no DOM or React code and returns `CalcResult | CalcError`. The UI branches on `result.valid` before reading any number, which removes the NaN and Infinity display bugs by construction. `calculate.test.ts` in Vitest covers normal doses, daily and single caps, missing concentration and invalid input.
- `calculateInfusion.ts` supports mcg/kg/min, mcg/kg/hr, mg/kg/hr and unit/kg/hr, and outputs a pump rate in mL/hr plus macro and micro drip rates. It shares only the input-validation pattern with the main engine.
- `suggest.ts` snaps doses to a `SOLID_FRACTIONS` array.
- The catalog lives in seven modules (analgesics, anti-infectives, antiparasitics, cardiorespiratory, emergency, gastro-allergy, steroids-nutrition) behind a barrel `index.ts`. The `DrugPreset` type separates calculation fields from display-only metadata.
- A per-dose / per-day entry toggle is saved and applied in both Preset and Puyer.
- History goes through `storage.ts`, a typed wrapper and the only code that touches `localStorage` directly. Custom presets, recents and favourites are also saved locally, keyed by a stable drug id.
- A dark-mode and accessibility pass added a medical-teal palette.
- GitHub Actions deploys to GitHub Pages. About 5,000 lines of TypeScript, 39 commits from 20 June to 13 July 2026.
